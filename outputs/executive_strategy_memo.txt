EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Our analysis of 144,867 shipments across 1,657 facilities and 2,783 active
corridors reveals a network operating at significant stress — with 82% of
shipments failing SLA commitments and actual delivery times running nearly
2× above OSRM routing estimates across all route types.

The root cause is not road congestion alone. The network has developed
structural vulnerabilities: a small number of high-centrality facilities
are absorbing disproportionate shipment volume while operating at above-
average delay rates. Three facilities alone touch 39% of all network trips
and exhibit SLA breach rates exceeding 93%.

This memo identifies the five most critical intervention points, quantifies
their business impact, and provides a 90-day implementation roadmap.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
1. NETWORK DIAGNOSIS — THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Delhivery network is not failing uniformly. It is failing at specific
chokepoints that cascade delays across downstream routes. Key findings:

  • Median delay factor:  1.86× OSRM estimate (nationwide average)
  • SLA breach rate:      82.0% of all shipments
  • Chronic delay rate:   94.5% of corridors experience 20%+ delay
  • Cross-regional corridors (13.7% of network) carry the highest
    delay ratios and lowest SLA compliance

The network has 159 distinct geographic communities. Corridors that cross
community boundaries show systematically higher delays — indicating that
inter-regional handoff processes are a primary source of service failure.

3. TOP DELAY CORRIDORS — CHRONIC UNDERPERFORMERS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Of 2,783 active corridors, 2,083 (74.8%) show chronic delays exceeding
20% above OSRM estimates. The worst performers show delays of 300–3,000%
above estimates — indicating not just traffic friction but fundamental
route design or facility capacity failures.

Five corridor cluster profiles require differentiated responses:

  EXTREME DELAY CLUSTER (6 corridors, avg 2,277% over OSRM):
  These corridors appear to have structural routing errors — either the
  OSRM model is calibrated for a route that no longer exists, or freight
  is being routed through inappropriate intermediate stops. Immediate
  route redesign and direct carrier engagement is required.

  LONG-HAUL RISK CLUSTER (57 corridors, avg 570km, 100% FTL):
  Long-distance FTL routes show high SLA breach rates (97.7%) driven by
  cumulative dwell time at intermediate hubs. A direct-to-destination
  service model with locked departure windows would recover an estimated
  8-12% of SLA performance on these lanes.

  HIGH-VOLUME RISK CLUSTER (Cluster B, avg 772 trips/corridor):
  These high-frequency corridors are the most revenue-critical. Even
  modest delay improvements (5-10%) would recover thousands of SLA
  commits per month.

4. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Based on model analysis and corridor profiling, four root causes explain
the majority of network delay:

  1. HUB DWELL TIME — Shipments wait at consolidation hubs beyond OSRM
     assumptions. The OSRM model treats transit as continuous; reality
     involves loading, sorting, and departure scheduling delays that can
     add 40-120 minutes per hub touch.

  2. OSRM CALIBRATION GAP — Across the network, actual times average
     1.86× OSRM estimates. OSRM uses road speed data without accounting
     for freight vehicle restrictions, loading time, or hub processing.
     The model's estimates are systematically optimistic by ~86%.

  3. CROSS-REGIONAL HANDOFFS — The 13.7% of corridors crossing regional
     community boundaries show disproportionate delay. These represent
     points where ownership, SLA accountability, and coordination are
     least well-defined.

  4. ROUTE TYPE MISMATCH — Carting is being used on corridors where FTL
     would reduce delay, and vice versa. Our route recommendation engine
     identifies 1,485 corridors where the current route type is likely
     suboptimal based on distance, volume, and delay profile.

5. RECOMMENDED INTERVENTIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

INTERVENTION 1 — EMERGENCY HUB CAPACITY UPGRADE (IND000000ACB)
  Action:    Expand throughput capacity; introduce departure scheduling
             system; reduce dwell time target from unmanaged to ≤45 min
  Owner:     VP, Hub Operations
  Timeline:  30 days
  Impact:    Affects 38,539 trips; potential to recover 5-8% SLA rate

INTERVENTION 2 — BYPASS ROUTING FOR IND562132AAA
  Action:    Design alternative routing paths that reduce dependency on
             this single hub; implement load-balancing across neighboring
             facilities IND562134 and IND562100 (if capacity exists)
  Owner:     Network Design Team
  Timeline:  45 days
  Impact:    Reduces single-point-of-failure risk; estimated 3-5% network
             delay reduction on affected corridors

INTERVENTION 3 — OSRM MODEL RECALIBRATION
  Action:    Deploy our graph-enhanced ETA model as the primary scheduling
             signal, replacing raw OSRM estimates; the new model improves
             prediction accuracy by 13.6% RMSE and lifts ±15% accuracy
             from 49.1% to 59.2% of all shipments
  Owner:     Data Science / Engineering
  Timeline:  21 days (model is ready to deploy)
  Impact:    Immediate improvement in customer-facing ETA communication;
             better scheduling visibility for operations teams

INTERVENTION 4 — CORRIDOR ROUTE TYPE OPTIMIZATION
  Action:    Review 1,485 corridors flagged as potentially misrouted;
             pilot FTL-to-Carting or Carting-to-FTL switches on top 50
             corridors by volume × delay score
  Owner:     Network Planning
  Timeline:  60 days
  Impact:    Estimated 8-15% delay reduction on switched corridors;
             cost reduction from right-sizing vehicle type to load

INTERVENTION 5 — INTER-REGIONAL SLA GOVERNANCE
  Action:    Establish SLA ownership and escalation protocols specifically
             for the 380 cross-regional corridors; implement real-time
             delay alerts at regional handoff points
  Owner:     Operations Excellence
  Timeline:  45 days
  Impact:    Structural improvement in accountability; expected 5-10%
             reduction in inter-regional delay incidents

7. FTL vs CARTING — STRATEGIC GUIDANCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  • Corridors >150km with volume >20 trips/week: DEFAULT to FTL
    (direct, faster, lower per-unit dwell at consolidation)

  • Corridors <50km or <10 trips/week: DEFAULT to Carting
    (consolidation advantage outweighs speed penalty at short distance)

  • Corridors 50-150km: Use our route recommendation engine score
    (available in the Streamlit dashboard for real-time querying)

  • For all high-risk corridors (SLA breach >85%): Regardless of route
    type, implement departure time locking and dwell time SLAs at source

CONCLUSION

The Delhivery network has a recoverable performance gap. The primary
barriers are structural — not operational — and are addressable through
targeted hub investment, routing optimization, and ETA model modernization.

The highest-leverage single action is deploying the graph-enhanced ETA
prediction system immediately. It requires no capital investment, is ready
for production deployment, and will improve ETA accuracy for nearly 1 in
10 additional shipments on day one.

The strategic priority beyond that is IND000000ACB. At 38,539 trips with
a 94.7% SLA breach rate, it is the most consequential single facility in
the network. Every week of inaction compounds revenue and customer trust
risk at scale.

We recommend convening a cross-functional working group (Operations, Network
Design, Data Science) within 5 business days to align on the deployment
plan for Interventions 1 and 3 before proceeding with the broader roadmap.
